# Thirst for Oxygen | Complete How to Play

> You are surrounded by the ocean, yet you thirst for oxygen.
>
> This is more than a control card. It is a field manual for the deep: movement, world rules, story, and the design logic behind the game. On your first dive, read **Remember These Six Things**. Return to the full guide whenever you want the complete picture.

---

## Remember These Six Things

| Your job | Short version |
| --- | --- |
| Move | Click the diver, drag to aim, and release to launch. |
| Stay alive | Oxygen is both a survival timer and a movement resource. At zero, the run fails. |
| Read the route | Water gravity keeps changing your speed. The straight guide line does not predict a curved flight path. |
| Fight | You can fight or route around danger. Every close approach costs risk. |
| Find oxygen | Torricelli Spaces are difficult-to-reach, repeatable breathing stations, not free central refills. |
| Understand the story | Pull the Abyss Core from the seabed, then carry it back to the surface while the world reacts. |

## Your First Dive: A Safe Learning Order

### Do not chase maximum distance first

The first trap is assuming that a longer pull always means better play. Every launch trades distance, speed, and resources. Start with short drags, watch how much momentum remains, and learn what the next Cell does before attempting a long shot. Find your first oxygen source, treat enemies as route costs rather than mandatory targets, and use each Checkpoint as a new practice start.

### The prologue is not a combat lesson

The three opening story cards are the world-building entrance. Click or press `Space` to advance. If the text is still typing, the first input reveals the full line and the second advances. You may skip the cards, but not the later gates and route events. Physics, enemies, combat, and the oxygen timer pause during the prologue.

The cards establish three facts: oxygen is collapsing, machines cannot approach the Core, and only living flesh can resonate with it.

> Do not memorise every number on your first run. Learn this rhythm: pull, release, read the water, find oxygen, then decide whether the fight is worth it.

## Controls and Elastic Movement

| Action | Input | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aim | Click the diver or an interactive area | Enters aim mode. |
| Set direction | Hold and drag | Drag direction sets the launch direction; distance sets launch strength. |
| Launch | Release | Applies one initial velocity; water, currents, and collisions then take over. |
| Read the prologue | Click or press `Space` | Advances story text only during the narrative cards. |
| Return | Use the page `Back` control | Returns to the home page without confusing the guide with a pause. |

The straight line is only a direction and pull-distance hint. It does not predict water gravity, currents, special acceleration, or the final stopping point.

```text
drag direction and distance
          ↓
set launch velocity v₀
          ↓
apply water, current, and special acceleration at fixed steps
          ↓
update velocity and position
          ↓
resolve bounce, blocking, and damage
          ↓
continue until the next launch or state change
```

```text
a_total = a_zone + a_current + a_special
v_next = clamp((v_current + a_total × Δt) × water_drag, -v_max, v_max)
p_next = p_current + v_next × Δt
```

- `a_zone` is the gravity of the current Cell.
- `a_current` is horizontal force from a current or environment.
- `a_special` is temporary force from a Boss, gravity field, or object.
- `water_drag` prevents endless drift in zero gravity.
- `v_max` keeps fast launches readable.

When oxygen or energy is low, use several short corrections instead of one maximum pull. Confirm the new Cell's gravity before committing to a long shot.

## Oxygen, Energy, Health, and Experience

| Resource | Purpose | When it is low or empty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oxygen O₂ | Survival timer and the core resource for deep-sea activity. | Zero oxygen fails the run and costs a permanent life. |
| Energy | Aim and launch pacing. | You must drift, attach, or wait before launching again. |
| Health | `0–100` damage pool for enemies and hazards. | Zero health costs a permanent life. |
| Lives | Three permanent attempts for the run. | When all are gone, the run ends permanently. |

Oxygen is currently a fixed-time resource: a full tank lasts about **40 seconds**. Energy begins recovering about one second after the last launch, even while the diver is still drifting. Natural health recovery checks the lower percentage of oxygen and energy: below 60% it does not start, at 60% it restores 2 HP per second, and at 80% it restores 5 HP per second.

Defeated enemies leave stationary experience orbs. You must safely reach an orb to collect it; winning the fight and collecting the reward are separate decisions.

| Enemy tier | Experience |
| --- | ---: |
| Tier 1 | 14 |
| Tier 2 | 24 |
| Tier 3 | 38 |
| Tier 4 | 58 |
| Mini Boss | 150 |
| Mutated Mini Boss | 220 |
| Final Boss | 600 |

At a level-up the game pauses and lets you choose a weapon or passive upgrade. A clean kill route can provide the full reward, but routing around an enemy is always legal.

## Water Gravity and Physics

Gravity belongs to the Cell, not to a small object placed in its centre. Entering a new Cell preserves current velocity; the next fixed physics step applies the new acceleration.

| Level | Visual and use | Acceleration | Player feel |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| `L3` | Dark water | `2.0G` downward | Fast descent and little correction time. |
| `L2` | Deep-blue transition | `1.5G` downward | Rising pressure between normal and high gravity. |
| `L1` | Standard blue water | `1.0G` downward | Baseline environment for learning launches. |
| `L0` | Neutral band | `0` | No vertical acceleration, but inertia remains. |
| `L-1` | Pale water | `1.0G` upward | Continuous ascent; the environment itself becomes an escape force. |

L0 does not stop a fast diver. A photosynthesis bubble removes temporary water acceleration but does not erase existing velocity. A collision changes the velocity vector, after which the current Cell's gravity still applies.

## Oxygen Sources: When to Risk a Route

Oxygen sources are route-planning tools, not decoration or free healing.

| Source | How to obtain it | Effect | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Torricelli Space | Reach a high, side, or turning route | Repeatable stable oxygen | You must leave the main fight and survive the return route. |
| Photosynthesis Bubble | Touch it | About `50 O₂` and temporary gravity immunity | Without gravity control, the diver can overshoot the route. |
| Oxygen Ore | Hit it with a high-speed body impact | Releases a limited oxygen supply | The impact and its position are dangerous. |
| Clear-Oxygen Bubble | Touch it | Immediate `25 O₂`, no gravity immunity | Single-use; Boss encounters regenerate it over time. |

Treat a Torricelli Space as an infinite breathing station at the end of a dangerous route. Check the return path first, approach with short launches, and use it as a route anchor before choosing to fight or retreat.

> The useful question is not “Where is the oxygen?” but “How much risk will I accept to reach it and return alive?”

## Environmental Objects and Route Reading

| Element | Behaviour | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coral cover | Small enemies stop pursuing and damage is avoided briefly. | Use it as a short route and breathing pause. |
| Spring Jelly | Reflects by the incidence angle and adds force. | Read the next gravity zone before choosing the entry angle. |
| Deep-Sea Mine | Causes a strong bounce and extra damage. | Never treat it like an ordinary wall. |
| Current | Applies horizontal force. | Let it move you, but reserve energy for correction. |
| Barrier or Spike Edge | Blocks passage; spikes also deal damage. | Use a new angle instead of a maximum-force collision. |
| Weight Stone | Falls naturally; a strong upward impact can break it. | Do not wait below it for too long. |
| Ink Zone | Limits the visible area around the diver. | Probe with short launches and rebuild the route from memory and HUD feedback. |
| Seaweed | Edge attachment that pauses gravity and allows energy recovery. | Treat it as a recovery stop. |
| Checkpoint | Updates the return point and refills health, oxygen, and energy. | It is a new route baseline, not an extra life. |
| Razor | Rotates around its centre; contact damages and displaces the diver. | Read its cycle and use its push instead of charging through it. |
| One-shot Button | Opens its assigned gates once. | Identify the target gate before committing to the new route. |

Conditional gates are full Cells using `L1` water rules. A button removes their chains; it does not silently replace their gravity. Read the route as: see the sealed gate, find the one-shot button, remember its target, return to the gate, then re-check oxygen and the surrounding L1 water.

## Combat, Enemies, and Bosses

Combat is one answer, not the only answer:

```text
explore → launch → spend oxygen and energy → find supply
      → fight or evade → collect experience → upgrade Build
      → clear the Boss → enter the next zone and face its aftermath
```

Ask three questions before a fight: Is the enemy blocking a required route or only offering experience? Can the experience orb be collected safely? Will the oxygen and energy cost leave enough for the next supply point?

The Descent teaches with Tier 1 creatures, develops the four Tier 2 core enemies, and then introduces Tier 3 elites that change the arena rules. The Ascent removes the gentle Tier 1 ecology, keeps the four core enemies, and brings back mutated Tier 4 versions plus split lanternfish and coral-back seahorses.

Every Boss room has at least one repeatable Torricelli Space in an upper, side, or turning route. Clear-Oxygen Bubbles appear during a Boss encounter. Bosses test oxygen, gravity, currents, collisions, and skill timing together, not damage numbers alone.

The Descent Bosses maintain a fragile seal and ecosystem. The Ascent keeps their familiar skills but adds **Aftermath**: contamination, oxygen loss, altered gravity, and enemy deaths chain together after the Core is removed.

The Final Boss, the Abyssal Sperm Whale, carries the ruins through the deep. Its pressure comes from summons, a revealed ruin, echo forms, abyssal sludge, gravity dominion, and corrupted oxygen rather than a simple health bar.

## Weapons and Build

The player starts at Lv.1 with **Knife Lv.1**. The current contract is three weapon slots, three passive slots, four candidates in each category, and a maximum of three active choices. The first, second, and third acquired choices are capped at `Lv.3`, `Lv.2`, and `Lv.1`; the Knife always occupies slot one.

| Weapon | Lv.1 | Lv.2 | Lv.3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Knife | Damages along the movement path. | Adds two side trails at 70% damage. | Deals a lingering area attack while stopped. |
| Trident | High-damage single ranged shot while stationary. | Hits stun the target. | A hit shortens the next cooldown. |
| Katana | Automatically swings at nearby enemies. | The next slash after movement deals double damage. | Sends a sword shockwave that can destroy enemy projectiles. |
| Light Machine Gun | Six shots locked to the first aim direction. | Shots four to six use distinct outlines. | All six rounds use distinct prism colours. |

The Machine Gun never retargets during a burst. The Katana is a separate instant arc slash, not a recoloured Knife trail.

| Passive | Solves | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oxygen Circulator | Oxygen cost and long routes | Endurance and distant supplies |
| Pressure Stabilizer | Aim, launch, and kill pacing | Consistent combat rhythm |
| Ecological Carapace | Ranged damage and resource-to-health conversion | Forgiveness and survival |
| Abyssal Amplifier | Weapon damage and clear speed | High-risk burst damage |

Choose the passive that changes your decisions rather than erasing the level rules. A Build is complete when both categories have no legal upgrade left.

## Checkpoints, Death, and Recovery

Touching a Checkpoint makes it the nearest return point and refills health, oxygen, and energy. It does not add a permanent life. Health reaching zero or oxygen reaching zero costs one life. If lives remain, the diver returns to the latest Checkpoint; after all three are spent, the run ends permanently.

After a death, ask whether you launched too far without confirming the next oxygen source, risked an experience orb that was not worth it, used the wrong gravity habit, forgot the Boss-room return path, or had a Build choice that could have made the route safer.

## The Two Chapter Arcs

### Descent

The first arc still has a fragile balance. Gravity and oxygen routes can be learned and predicted:

```text
Entrance → Deep-Sea Forest → Thermal Vents → Abyssal Ruins
         → Mini Boss A → Mini Boss B
```

It teaches the difference between Tier 1 confidence, Tier 2 attack patterns, Tier 3 arena rules, and the reason stable oxygen is placed on difficult return routes.

### Ascent

Removing the Core reopens the seabed wound. The Ascent is not a reversed replay; it is the same world reacting to injury:

```text
Abyssal Ruins → Trench → Coral Zone → Shallow Sea
              → Mini Boss C → Mini Boss D → Final Boss
              → surface with the Abyss Core
```

Friendly objects become rarer, Tier 1 teaching creatures disappear, core Tier 2 enemies remain, and mutated elites return. Oxygen sources become high-risk route anchors. The feeling should be “the world is sick and its life-support rules are now hunting you,” not simply “the numbers are larger.”

## What the Story Is About

Humanity once fought an unknown civilisation. That civilisation placed the **Abyss Core** in the deepest trench to seal the planet's largest seabed fissure. The Core stopped the leak but released radiation that rewrote evolution: algae died, oxygen-producing ecology collapsed, and the world slowly forgot how to breathe.

The Core rejects AI, robots, submarines, and intelligent machinery. Only a living nervous system can cross its resonance boundary.

The protagonist is a deep-sea thief and an atoner for humanity's debt, not a spotless hero. The Descent reaches the ruins and removes the Core. The Ascent carries it away while pressure, contamination, mutations, and the awakened Abyssal Sperm Whale pursue the diver.

> The way to save the ocean is not to carry oxygen down. It is to pull out the heart that made the ocean forget how to breathe, then accept the cost of that choice.

| Opening card | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Oxygen collapse | This is a world-wide failure, not one city's accident. |
| The Abyss Core and the machine boundary | Steel cannot approach the Core. |
| The living diver and the signal | Only living flesh can cross the resonance. |

The Descent feels ancient, complete, and rhythmic. Removing the Core feels like a pulled heart and a broken acoustic structure. The Ascent keeps the same motifs but adds detuned pulses, broken echoes, and unstable breathing.

## How Sound, Visuals, and the HUD Explain the World

The style is ancient acoustic engineering: stone carving, shells, bone-like structures, and seawater forming one resonant system. Geometry uses regular pulses; bio-mechanics use breathing, low calls, shell impacts, and friction; erosion uses deep resonance and cracks; cyan fluorescence uses rare high harmonics.

Three musical phases support the read of the world:

1. **Sleep and detection** — sparse phrases and single breaths.
2. **Symbiosis and pressure** — short pulses answer one another and Boss versions become ritual layers.
3. **Core and imbalance** — missing beats, fractured harmonics, and unstable breath.

The HUD prioritises health, oxygen, energy, and combat focus; then route danger; then first-contact cards; then story flavour. The full Markdown manual is for reading outside combat. In the game, a first-contact card gives only the shortest useful reminder.

## Encyclopedia, Map Editor, and the Public GDD Index

The World Encyclopedia is the quick reference for enemies, map elements, weapons, passives, and Resonance. The Map Editor is a hexagonal rule surface:

| Layer | Contains | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cell | Terrain and water gravity | `L3`, `L2`, `L1`, `L0`, `L-1`, blocked terrain |
| Cell Overlay | An effect over one Cell or a region | Coral cover, Ink Zone |
| Cell Object | A free-position object on a Cell face | Mine, Weight Stone, Seaweed, oxygen source, Checkpoint, Button |
| Edge | Interaction between neighbouring Cells | Spring Jelly, Spike, Current |
| Actor | A moving role attached to the map | Player, enemy, Mini Boss, Final Boss, spawn point |

Water rules use **Cell Snap**, ordinary objects use **Free Snap**, and edge interactions use **Edge Snap**. Those meanings are why a Torricelli Space can sit at a turning side route instead of being forced into the centre of a Cell.

The full design source remains organised in `GDD/01_Vision` through `GDD/09_Management`; the game and its adjustable data modules are the authority for currently playable values.

## Version Notes: Confirmed and Still Tuning

### Playable rules

- Drag direction and distance set launch velocity.
- Cell water gravity applies continuously from `L3` to `L-1`.
- Oxygen lasts about 40 seconds at a full tank.
- Photosynthesis Bubbles restore about 50 O₂ and grant temporary gravity immunity.
- Clear-Oxygen Bubbles restore 25 O₂ and regenerate during Boss encounters.
- Boss rooms place a repeatable Torricelli Space on an upper, side, or turning route.
- Health is 0–100; the run begins with three permanent lives and returns to Checkpoints.
- The Knife is the fixed starter; the current complete Build spans Lv.1 through Lv.12.

### Content still tuning

Some enemy art, Boss animation, HUD fields, platform controls, Actor spawn rules, object overlap limits, and final ending presentation are still being tested. The guide keeps those items marked as tuning instead of presenting a proposal as a promise.

> Use the in-game HUD, current data modules, and actually playable rules as the authority. A GDD proposal remains a proposal until it is implemented and tested.

## Final Dive Cheat Sheet

```text
Check oxygen first, then the route.
Read the water before you pull.
Plan the return before touching a supply.
Ask whether the fight is worth its cost.
When you see a Torricelli Space, do not assume it is central; it is usually hidden on the hard route.
After the Core is removed, the world will not reward you. It will chase you.
```

What you are learning is not how to launch the diver the farthest. You are learning how to make the next launch meaningful in a world that keeps changing gravity, oxygen, and ecology.
